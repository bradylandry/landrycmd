01 · the method I built

Move traffic by winning an election, not by cutting over

A gateway migration normally means shutting the old device and enabling the new one. That couples the traffic move to the physical move, which is why those windows drop packets and why rollback means re-cabling at 2am.

I built a different method: a throwaway L2 bridge puts both vendors’ routers in one redundancy group, so the new router sits as the deliberate loser of the election carrying zero traffic. Then traffic moves when a priority value changes. Nothing is ever touched while it is forwarding, and rollback at any step is one value. I ran it across 14 markets.

Below: switch between Hardcut and Straddle and watch the packets-lost counter. The scenario pills inject real failures — a gateway MAC that changed, preemption left enabled, an edge-port misconfiguration on the bridge.
