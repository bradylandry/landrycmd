01 · the decision

The ring was not legacy cruft — it was there for a reason

Each metro market ran two topologies at once: a hub-and-spoke carrying all unicast, and a separate 10G ring carrying video multicast only. That looks redundant until you ask why. It kept video off the unicast path, and because the ring carried exactly one service it needed almost no QoS engineering — there was nothing to protect video from.

Collapsing it means accepting that QoS burden on every shared link, forever. The win is one topology to operate instead of two. That is the real trade, and it is invisible if you only count bandwidth.

The sequencing is the whole lesson. Capacity went in four months before the ring was touched, and every network in one area was advertised into the other and validated — traffic and video — while the ring was still carrying it. The links came out last.
